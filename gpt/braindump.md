# Brain Dump

* "do we need encoders to understand the prompt?" in what i built in `gpt-dev3.2` with that `torch.tril` mask - no. for ChatGPT style models - no. they just use a single decoder to handle both reading the prompt and generating the text.
* "are Q, K, V weights generated uniquely for each token?" turns out - no. in `gpt-dev3.2` the `Linear` layers are totally shared. every single token in the sequence hits the exact same weight matrices. they only output different vectors because their starting 32-bit embeddings were different.
* "why mask the prompt and predict early words if we just want the response?" this is the prefill phase. the massive parallel math i learned in `gpt-dev1.ipynb` processes the whole prompt at once. we mask it cause a decoder only knows how to look backward. it actually does predict all the early words in the prompt but we literally throw them in the trash and only use the very last one to get the first brand new generated word.
* "can autoregressive generation be parallelized?" no. my `generate` loop in `gpt-dev2.13.ipynb` is slow cause it blindly recalculates the past for every single new word. to fix it we use a KV cache. we save the K and V activations (the actual payload, not weights) from the prefill phase into gpu memory. then the loop only computes Q, K, V for the one new token and compares its Q to the cached K.
* "does vLLM save K and V anywhere to solve fragmentation?" yep. normal KV caching wastes a ton of space by pre-allocating giant continuous blocks just in case. vLLM borrows OS virtual memory tricks to chop the cache into small pages scattered anywhere in the GPU. it allocates them dynamically token by token so no space is wasted and way more users can run on the same hardware.
* "i know in training... there is loss function, backprop, regularization". exactly. but in inference, we rip all that out. there is no loss function and no backprop. you are just doing forward passes.
* for your prompt "what is fullform of GPU?", prefill does its massive parallel math on that 5x32 matrix. it spits out raw scores (logits) for all 5 positions. like we said earlier, we throw the first 4 in the trash. we take the logits for the 5th position (the "GPU?" token), run it through a softmax to get probabilities, and pick the winning word. let's say it predicts "Graphics".
* "but I don't know what will happen during inference." here is the exact hand-off. before we move on to generate the next word, we take the K and V matrices that were just calculated for those 5 prompt tokens and permanently save them into the GPU memory. this is your KV cache getting initialized.
* now the autoregressive loop starts. we take our brand new token "Graphics". we grab its 32-bit embedding and add its position vector (position 6). so now we just have a tiny 1x32 vector passing through the network. we multiply it by the shared $W_Q$, $W_K$, $W_V$ weights to get a single Q, K, and V vector for this one word.
* here is where the cache magic happens. we take that single new Q vector and multiply it against the *cached* K matrix (which holds the identity of the 5 prompt tokens + the 1 new token). this gives us the attention scores—basically asking "how much should 'Graphics' care about 'fullform' or 'GPU'?". we multiply those scores by the cached V matrix to get the context payload.
* that payload goes through the feed-forward network, spits out new logits, goes through softmax, and picks the next word. let's say it picks "Processing". we save the K and V for "Graphics" into the cache, and start the loop all over again for "Processing". it just loops like this, one token at a time, until it eventually predicts a special `<|endoftext|>` token, and then it stops and shows you the answer.
* "shared Wq, Wk, Wv? is that weights of the model?" yes, exactly. in your micrograd code, these are literally the Linear layers you defined inside your self-attention module. they don't belong to any specific token. they belong to the neural network itself. these are the exact parameters that were learned during training and are now completely frozen for inference.
* "if weights of the model don't change --> what's actually changing?" the input. matrix multiplication is just input * weight = output . the weight matrix is identical for everyone, but the 32-bit input vector hitting it is unique. the embedding for "Graphics" is mathematically completely different from "GPU". when you multiply a unique input vector by a shared weight matrix, you get a completely unique output vector.
* "are the key, query and value vectors also same for each token beforehand similar to embedding?" no. embeddings are static (they just sit in a lookup table beforehand). Q, K, and V do *not* exist beforehand. they are computed dynamically on the fly during the forward pass. the model takes that static 32-bit embedding, runs it through the $W_Q$ linear layer, and the mathematical result of that multiplication *is* the Query vector. it runs it through $W_K$ to create the Key, and $W_V$ to create the Value.
* "why did my makemore code only have one W?" in makemore (like a simple bigram), a token just looks at itself and blindly predicts the next word. input goes through W and out comes a prediction. simple. but in gpt , tokens need to read the whole sentence and share context with each other *before* they try to guess the next word.
* "so how do tokens share context, and what is V?" to share context, the attention mechanism splits the problem into two distinct steps: finding out *who* to listen to, and figuring out *what* they are actually saying. Q and K only handle the "who".
* when you multiply Q and K and run softmax in your `gpt-dev3.2` notebook, the output is literally just a grid of percentages. it's just routing instructions. it tells the network something like: "token 5 needs to get 80% of its context from token 2, and 20% from token 4."
* "but 80% of what?" that is exactly where V comes in. you don't want token 5 to just absorb token 2's raw 32-bit embedding, because that embedding is doing other jobs. instead, token 2's embedding is multiplied by $W_V$ to create a dedicated "message" vector. this is V.
* the final payoff: if the Q and K routing system decides token 5 pays 80% attention to token 2, token 5 literally takes 0.8 multiplied by token 2's V vector, and adds it to its own context. Q and K are just the plumbing routing the water. V is the actual water flowing through the pipes.
* "are V and embedding somewhat similar?" yes, because they are both just lists of numbers (vectors). but they do totally different jobs. the 32-bit embedding is a static dictionary lookup. the raw embedding for "bat" is always the exact same 32 numbers, whether you are playing baseball or exploring a cave. it's a muddy mashup of *every* possible meaning—it has a little bit of the "sports" dimension and a little bit of the "animal" dimension turned on. V is a dynamic message computed on the fly specifically to share context.
* "my intuition is that context for bat comes from 'like to' in sentence 1, and 'fear' in sentence 2. is that right?" your intuition is 100% dead on. here is exactly how Q, K, and V make that happen intuitively.
* "what does the Query do here?" when the model reaches the word "bat" (or "bats"), it takes that static, muddy embedding and multiplies it by $W_Q$. the resulting Query vector is basically a beacon yelling: "I am the word 'bat', I am highly ambiguous, I need clues! Does anyone behind me have context about sports or scary animals?"
* "what do the Keys do?" while "bat" is yelling its Query, the previous words are holding up their Keys. in sentence 1, the words "like" and "to" have Keys that say: "we are talking about hobbies and actions." in sentence 2, the word "fear" holds up a Key that says: "i am a scary/danger emotion."
* "how does the matchmaking actually route the context?" we do the dot product. the Query of "bat" compares itself to the Key of "fear". it's a massive mathematical match. the attention score shoots up to 90%. the network just decided that "bat" needs to listen almost exclusively to "fear".
* "so what is V's role in the 'fear bats' sentence?" if Q and K just decided that "bat" needs to listen to "fear", V is what "fear" actually says back. the word "fear" took its own embedding, multiplied it by $W_V$, and created a payload (Value) that basically says: "Here is a bunch of data about scary, dark, animal-related stuff."
* "what is the final result?" because of that 90% attention score, the word "bat" literally absorbs 90% of the V payload from "fear". so as "bat" exits the self-attention layer, it is no longer a muddy mashup of sports and animals. it has been mathematically updated by the context. its "animal" dimensions are now glowing red hot, and its "sports" dimensions are completely suppressed.
